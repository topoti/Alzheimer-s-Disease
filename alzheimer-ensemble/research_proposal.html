Research proposal · MRI / Alzheimer staging

Staging Alzheimer's disease from brain MRI, measured per patient

A deep transfer learning study on OASIS structural MRI, designed so that every reported number describes performance on a patient the model has never seen. The experimental protocol is the contribution.

Dataset: OASIS-1, 347 subjects Task: 3-class staging Compute: Kaggle free T4 Status: pipeline built, results pending
§ 00the one-paragraph version

What I propose to build

I am building a classifier that reads a structural brain MRI and assigns it to one of three stages: no dementia, very mild dementia, or mild-or-worse dementia. The approach is transfer learning — take a convolutional network already trained to see on millions of ordinary photographs, and retrain its decision layer for this medical task, because 347 patients is nowhere near enough data to teach a network to see from scratch.

The modelling is standard and I expect it to work. The part of this project that requires real care, and where I am asking for your agreement before I commit GPU time, is the experimental protocol: how the data is partitioned, what unit performance is measured on, and which number gets reported as the result.

The proposition

Build a stage classifier whose headline metric is computed per patient, on patients held out entirely from training, benchmarked against the score a model achieves by learning nothing at all. I expect a modest number. I would rather defend a modest number that holds than report a large one that does not.

§ 01the clinical problem

Why staging, and why MRI

Alzheimer's disease progresses gradually, and the window in which intervention is most useful falls early — before substantial cognitive decline is apparent. Structural MRI records the physical correlates of that progression: cortical thinning, hippocampal and medial temporal atrophy, and enlargement of the ventricles as surrounding tissue is lost.

These changes are visible, but in the early stages they are subtle and their assessment is time-consuming and subject to inter-rater variability. That makes the problem a reasonable candidate for automation: a model that flags likely early-stage cases for closer human review is useful even when it is not independently reliable.

The scientifically interesting and clinically important boundary is not healthy vs. advanced dementia — that distinction is comparatively easy and comparatively useless, because by then the diagnosis is not in doubt. It is the boundary between healthy and very mild. My evaluation is therefore designed to make that specific confusion visible rather than averaging it away.

§ 02the data, counted honestly

What the dataset actually contains

The corpus is OASIS-1 cross-sectional MRI. Each brain volume was sliced along the z-axis and slices 100–160 retained as images. This produces a file count that is badly misleading about how much data there really is.

The number that governs this project

86,437 images sounds like a large dataset. It is not. It is 347 people. Every patient was scanned about four times and each scan sliced ~61 ways, so one person contributes roughly 240 near-identical pictures of the same skull. Those 240 images are one sample, not 240. Everything in this proposal follows from taking that seriously.

Structure verified directly against the files

The filename encodes the grouping key: OAS1_0351_MR1_mpr-4_160.jpg gives patient OAS1_0351, session MR1, acquisition mpr-4, slice 160. I checked three things before designing anything:

  • No patient appears in more than one class. OASIS-1 assigns a single clinical dementia rating per subject, so a patient belongs to exactly one stage. Cross-class contamination is ruled out.
  • Slice coverage is uniform — exactly 1,417 images at each of the 61 slice indices.
  • Repeat scans are the norm. Patients carry ~4 mpr acquisitions of the same head, so (patient, slice) is not a unique key — it collides on 21,167 of the 86,437 files. Any code that assumes one scan per person will silently miscount.

Why three classes and not four

The source data separates Mild from Moderate dementia. Moderate contains two patients. Two people cannot be meaningfully present in both a training and a testing partition, and any accuracy figure reported for that class would describe those two individuals rather than the condition. I therefore merge Moderate into Mild and report a three-class task, stating the merge explicitly so a reader knows what the third class contains.

Design decision

Three classes: no dementia (266 patients) / very mild (58) / mild-or-worse (23). A binary formulation — demented vs. not — is held in reserve as a fallback should the three-class result prove too unstable to interpret.

§ 03the failure mode I am designing against

The central methodological risk

This dataset contains a trap that will silently produce an excellent-looking and entirely worthless result, and avoiding it determines the whole design.

Suppose I pool all 86,437 images and shuffle them randomly into 80% training and 20% testing. Slice 120 of a given brain goes into training; slice 121 of that same brain goes into testing. The two pictures are nearly identical — same skull shape, same head size, same scanner framing, one slice apart.

What the model learns instead

Under that split the network does not need to learn what atrophy looks like. It can score extremely well by recognising individuals — memorising skull geometry during training and matching it at test time. The test score then measures the model's ability to re-identify people it has already seen, which is a problem it was never asked to solve and which has no clinical value whatsoever. The resulting number can look superb while the model is useless on a new patient.

This is not a hypothetical concern. It is the dominant failure mode for any dataset where one subject contributes many correlated samples, and the resulting inflation is large — large enough that a leaked result and an honest result are not on the same scale and cannot be compared.

The countermeasure, stated as a rule

Split by patient, never by image. All slices and all repeat acquisitions of one person stay on the same side of every partition, enforced mechanically via StratifiedGroupKFold with the patient ID as the grouping key. And evaluate per patient, not per image: aggregate a patient's slice predictions into one prediction for that person, because the person is the unit a clinician makes a decision about.

Grouping by patient ID also handles the repeat-scan problem automatically — all four acquisitions of one head travel together, so the model is never tested on a second photograph of a brain it studied.

§ 04models, smallest first

Which models I intend to use

The governing constraint is 347 patients. A network's capacity to memorise scales with its parameter count, so with a sample this small the discipline is to start at the smallest model that could plausibly work and only climb if the data earns it. Each rung below must beat the one beneath it on held-out patients before I move up.

Majority-class baselinemandatory floor Predict "no dementia" for every patient. Learns nothing, yet scores 76.7% accuracy because 266 of 347 patients are healthy. Printed in every results table so that no accuracy figure can be read without its floor beside it.
Cached-feature linear probecheap reference Freeze the pretrained network entirely, extract each image's features once, and train only a linear classifier on top. Runs in seconds per fold, which makes it affordable to test design choices across all five folds instead of guessing from one.
ResNet-18 — primary model11 M params ImageNet-pretrained, fine-tuned in two stages. Small enough to resist memorising 221 training patients while still being a genuine convolutional network. This is the workhorse.
EfficientNet-B0 / ResNet-50capacity test Second and third backbones, run only once ResNet-18 is stable. These answer an explicit question — does additional capacity help at this sample size, or does it only accelerate memorisation?
Probability-averaging ensembleif justified Average the predicted probabilities of the individual backbones. Ensembling usually buys stability, which matters when single-fold scores are noisy — but it is tested at patient level and only once the members individually clear the baseline.

Input representation: 2.5D rather than grey×3

Pretrained networks expect a three-channel colour image, while MRI slices are greyscale. The conventional fix copies one grey slice into all three channels, which means two-thirds of every input carries no information at all.

Instead I stack three adjacent slices into the three channels — s−2, s, s+2, with the centre slice carrying the label. The network can then see how a structure changes as you move through the head, which is genuine anatomical context, for zero additional parameters. Neighbouring slices are drawn from the full scan before any slice-window filtering, so even edge slices get real neighbours rather than a duplicated centre; this was verified across 36,842 lookups with no fallbacks. Augmentation is applied to the merged three-channel image so that a rotation moves all three slices identically and the anatomy stays registered.

Training recipe

Two-stage transfer. Stage one freezes the pretrained body and trains only the newly attached classifier head for 3 epochs — the fresh head starts out random, and letting its early wild corrections propagate backwards would damage the pretrained features. Stage two unfreezes the top block and fine-tunes everything at a much lower rate for up to 12 epochs, early-stopped on validation macro-F1, keeping the best checkpoint rather than the last.

Settings: AdamW; head learning rate 1e-3 then 2e-4, backbone 2e-5 (discriminative rates with cosine decay); weight decay 1e-2; dropout 0.5; label smoothing 0.05; batch size 32; 224×224 inputs; central slices 124–136.

Class imbalance is handled by a class-weighted loss and a weighted batch sampler, so the rare dementia classes are not drowned out by the healthy majority. I am deliberately not using ADASYN or SMOTE: those methods interpolate between feature vectors, and interpolating between raw brain images produces blurred composites that correspond to no real anatomy.

Augmentation applies to the training split only: rotation ±10–15°, small shifts and zooms, brightness and contrast jitter, random erasing, and horizontal flips (brains are approximately symmetric). No vertical flips and no 90° rotations — a head never presents upside-down in a scanner, so such examples teach the model about images that will never occur at test time.

§ 05the pipeline, in order

Method, step by step

These steps are strictly ordered. Each depends on the one before it, and the ordering is what keeps the result honest — in particular, partitioning happens before augmentation and before any model is fitted.

  1. Parse patient IDs from filenamesBuild a table of image_path → patient_id → class. The patient ID is the grouping key for everything downstream.
  2. Merge Moderate into MildProduce three-class labels while retaining the original folder name, so the two Moderate patients stay traceable in the published split file.
  3. Lock away a held-out test set of patients20% of patients, stratified by class — 70 people. Marked fold = −1 and not inspected again until the recipe is frozen.
  4. StratifiedGroupKFold on the remainder277 development patients into 5 folds of 55–56, grouped by patient ID. No person's data appears in two folds.
  5. Restrict to central slicesKeep slices 124–136 rather than all 61. Redundancy across neighbouring slices is high and the central window carries the signal; widened to 118–142 if the model proves to be memorising.
  6. Augment and rebalance inside the training split onlyNever on validation or test — those must consist of real, unmodified scans as they would actually arrive.
  7. Two-stage transfer learning, per foldHead-only, then partial unfreeze. Early-stop on validation macro-F1 and retain the best checkpoint.
  8. Aggregate slice predictions to one per patientAverage the predicted probabilities across all of a patient's slices and acquisitions, then take the argmax. One prediction per person.
  9. Report mean ± standard deviation across the 5 foldsMacro-F1, balanced accuracy, per-class recall and confusion matrix — always printed beside the majority baseline.
  10. Grad-CAM audit, then the locked test set onceConfirm the model attends to brain tissue, freeze the recipe, then evaluate the 70 held-out patients a single time. That number is the headline result.

Why not accuracy

266 of 347 patients are non-demented. A model that outputs "no dementia" unconditionally — that has learned nothing — scores 76.7% accuracy while catching not a single case of dementia. Accuracy in this problem is therefore close to meaningless on its own, and I will not use it as a headline figure.

The primary metric is macro-F1 at patient level, which scores each class separately and averages them, so ignoring the rare dementia classes is heavily penalised. Secondary metrics are balanced accuracy, per-class recall, the confusion matrix, and per-class precision–recall AUC. The majority baseline appears as a row in every results table.

How noisy a single fold really is

A validation fold of 56 patients breaks down to roughly 42 / 10 / 4 across the three classes. With four patients in the rarest class, one patient changing answer moves macro-F1 by about 0.05. I will therefore not conclude that a change helped from a single fold moving 0.02 — every reported comparison runs all five folds and reports a standard deviation.

Reproducibility

A fixed global seed across Python, NumPy and PyTorch with deterministic cuDNN. The fold assignment of every patient is written to a versioned CSV and published alongside the results, so the partition itself is auditable — a reader can verify that no patient crossed a boundary rather than taking my word for it.

§ 07explanations with a job to do

Explainability

A model that outputs a dementia stage without justification is not clinically usable, regardless of its score — a clinician needs to see what drove the decision in order to decide whether to trust it. I will produce Grad-CAM heatmaps and gradient saliency maps over correctly and incorrectly classified patients, showing which regions of the scan most influenced the prediction.

Beyond that conventional use, these visualisations serve a second and in my view more valuable function in this project:

Grad-CAM as a leakage detector

A model that is scoring well by recognising individuals will tend to attend to the skull outline, the scalp, and scanner framing. A model that is genuinely detecting atrophy should attend to brain tissue — the ventricles and medial temporal structures. Running Grad-CAM is therefore an independent, cheap check on whether the protective measures in §03 actually worked. If the heat lands on the skull, something is wrong with my data handling, not with the model.

The first run failed, and the failure is informative

An early configuration produced this trace:

stage 2 · epoch 20.25900.43650.4357

Training loss fell threefold while the validation score fell every single epoch, finishing at 0.68 accuracy — below the 0.767 a model scores by guessing "no dementia" every time. That is the textbook signature of overfitting: 11 million adjustable parameters memorising 221 people rather than learning what dementia looks like.

I report this deliberately. It is the clearest practical evidence I can offer that the protocol works as intended — a patient-level split exposed this failure immediately, where an image-level split would have concealed it behind a high score and I would still believe the model worked.

The response has been to constrain capacity rather than add it: widen the slice window for more genuine data, freeze a larger share of the network during fine-tuning, pin batch-normalisation statistics in frozen blocks, strengthen augmentation, and raise weight decay a hundredfold alongside dropout and label smoothing. Those changes are in place and being re-measured.

§ 09what success looks like

Expected outcome, stated in advance

I am committing to this expectation before running the experiments, so the criterion cannot be retrofitted to whatever happens to come out:

Pre-registered expectation

A patient-level macro-F1 in the range 0.55–0.70, clearly above the majority-baseline floor. If any configuration returns above 0.90, my first action will be to search for a leak in the pipeline — not to report it.

That second sentence is the part I would most like noted. On a dataset of 347 people with 23 in the rarest class, a near-perfect score is far more likely to indicate a broken experiment than a breakthrough, and treating it as a red flag rather than a result is a deliberate commitment of this project.

The deliverables are threefold:

  • A leakage-safe benchmark for three-class OASIS staging, measured per patient, that a later study can build on rather than having to re-derive.
  • A controlled comparison of backbone capacity, and of the 2.5D input against the conventional greyscale-replicated input — each tested across all five folds.
  • A published, auditable split file recording which patient sat in which partition, so the central methodological claim can be verified by a reader instead of trusted.
§ 10stated plainly, not buried

Risks and limitations

  • Single-site data. OASIS-1 only, with no external validation cohort. Generalisation beyond this scanner and this population is untested, and I will not claim it.
  • Small effective sample. 347 people, 23 of them in the rarest class. Confidence intervals will be wide and will be reported as such.
  • 2D slices discard volumetric context. The 2.5D input mitigates this; it does not eliminate it. A full 3D approach is out of reach at this sample size and compute budget.
  • Moderate dementia is not separately assessable at two patients, and is merged rather than claimed.
  • Imaging only. No cognitive scores, demographics or genetic markers are fused in, though they would likely help considerably. This is a scope decision, not an oversight.
  • The reported score will be modest by the standards of headline figures in this application area. That is the expected and intended consequence of the protocol in §03.

Stated openly, these strengthen rather than weaken the work: they show the failure modes were identified in advance and designed around, which is exactly the claim the project is making.

§ 11the ask

What I need agreed

  • The three-class formulation, with Moderate merged into Mild and the merge disclosed, rather than a four-class staging that would require reporting on a two-patient class.
  • Patient-level macro-F1 as the headline metric, with accuracy appearing only beside its majority baseline.
  • The 0.55–0.70 target range, so that a methodologically correct result is not mistaken for a disappointing one when set against the larger figures common in this area.
  • Compute: Kaggle's free T4 allocation is sufficient. No local GPU is available; the cached-feature probe is what keeps five-fold sweeps inside that quota.

With those agreed, the remaining work is the full cross-validation run, the Grad-CAM audit, and the single held-out evaluation — in that order.
